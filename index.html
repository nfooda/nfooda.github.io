About

I'm a Ph.D. student at the University of Maryland, advised by Prof. Dave Levin. I work on Internet measurement and network security, with a focus on the Web PKI: how quickly certificate revocations actually reach browsers, how Firefox checks revocation at scale with CRLite, and how privacy technologies like DNS over HTTPS and Encrypted Client Hello are used in practice. My work has appeared at ACM SIGCOMM and ACM IMC.

Before my Ph.D., I worked as a software engineer intern at Siemens, building C/C++ tooling and internal dashboards, and I have hands-on experience in full-stack development, hardware security, and applied ML/NLP.

Research

Certificate Revocation LatencyIMC 2026 · extension ongoing
  • Built a framework that issues certificates, revokes them immediately, and polls each CA's OCSP responder and CRL every minute, alongside Mozilla's CRLite snapshots, until the revocation appears.
  • Ran about 3,000 trials over 22 days against six CAs that together account for over 75% of Firefox certificate validations.
  • All six CAs met their Baseline Requirements deadlines, yet clients stay exposed for hours or days because of pre-signed OCSP responses, cached CRLs, and filter rebuild schedules.
  • Now extending the study to every CA with an automated revocation interface, regional CAs, and revocations requested by portal, email, or phone.

CRLite Deployment in Mozilla FirefoxSIGCOMM 2026
  • Evaluated CRLite, the on-device revocation system deployed to all Firefox desktop users, in collaboration with Mozilla engineers.
  • Using 90 days of Firefox telemetry and production logs, found CRLite covers 87.63% of real certificate validations, and traced nearly all of the rest to update windows and ingest delays.
  • Measured filter update costs during the 2025 Microsoft mass revocation and simulated revoking half of all certificates, showing the filter recovers within a week under short certificate lifetimes.

Measuring Web Privacy from the User's Sideongoing
  • Built a Firefox extension and server back end that record, per site visited, whether the connection uses encrypted DNS, Encrypted Client Hello (ECH), and OCSP-free revocation checking.
  • Designed for participant privacy: only per-site aggregate counts, no browsing history, and nothing uploaded without consent.
  • Validating its measurements against Firefox's network logs ahead of an IRB-approved deployment to about 200 participants.
